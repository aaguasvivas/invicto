How to run the table: the official strategy guide

This guide is not forum speculation: it is written from the game engine itself, with the exact rules that decide whether your team goes 7-0 or dies in the quarterfinals. If you want the perfect season, this is what actually matters.

1. Your team is worth its weakest category

Your team rating is not a simple average. The engine evaluates six categories (contact hitting, power, speed, defense, clutch, and your three pitchers as a group) and blends them with a soft minimum: weak categories weigh far more than brilliant ones. In plain English: a lineup with five categories at 90 and one at 40 does not play like an 82 team. It plays much closer to that 40 than you think.

The practical consequence is rule number one of Invicto: never punt anything. A single hole (the classic "I will grab any closer and move on") puts a ceiling on the entire run. Before you chase a third batting superstar, patch the ugliest hole on the team.

2. Pitching is a full category, not a formality

SP, RP and CL are only 3 of your 12 picks, but they are worth one full sixth of the team rating, under the same soft-minimum rule. A punted staff sinks you even if you hit like the 2013 Dominicans. Elite starters (think 2006 Johan Santana or 2026 Paul Skenes) are gold, and the RP slot accepts any pitcher, so use it to fit the best leftover arm you have, starter or reliever.

3. Powerhouses appear on 7 of every 10 rolls: wait for them

The engine gives the ten powerhouse countries (Dominican Republic, Venezuela, Puerto Rico, United States, Japan, Mexico, Cuba, South Korea, Chinese Taipei and the Netherlands) about 70% of all rolls. That changes the math: if a thin roster comes up in round 2, do not settle "just in case". The odds that the next roll brings a powerhouse are on your side.

4. Your two rerolls are the real strategy

You get one country reroll (same edition) and one edition reroll (same country) per run. How you spend them separates good runs from perfect ones:

  • Use the country reroll when a roster has nothing good at the positions you still need. If nobody on it fits at all, the game rolls again for free and you keep your reroll.
  • Use the edition reroll when the country is right but the year is wrong. Rolled Venezuela 2026 with your infield already set? The reroll gives you another Venezuela year at random, and a year like 2006 brings Johan Santana and Rafael Betancourt for your staff.
  • Do not save your rerolls for the end. The team rating depends only on your final 12, not on the round you drafted them in: a weak pick in round 2 costs as much as one in round 11, and an unused reroll is worth nothing when the draft ends. Spend one as soon as a roll is clearly worse than what you usually get for the positions you still need.

5. Learn where the talent lives by position

After a few runs you will see the patterns the engine pulls from real data. Every United States roster brings a good catcher (Cal Raleigh, Jonathan Lucroy, Brian McCann), and Venezuela is next. The luxury shortstops come from the Dominican Republic (Hanley Ramírez) and the United States (Bobby Witt Jr.), and so does most of the raw power. If you need a closer, the United States brings an elite one almost every edition (Joe Nathan, Craig Kimbrel, Andrew Miller); elsewhere, look for Puerto Rico from 2017 on (Edwin Díaz), the 2013 Dominicans (Fernando Rodney) and the 2017 Dutch (Kenley Jansen). When that flag comes up, you already know which position to fill.

6. In Hard Mode, memory is your rating

Hard Mode hides the numbers, but the ratings are still down there deciding games. The edge belongs to whoever recognizes names: if you know that 2006 Lee Seung-yeop was a home run machine or that 2013 Hong-Chih Kuo was untouchable, you can build an elite team without seeing a single number. Studying the rosters is literally training for this mode.

7. The math of perfection

Do not let a 6-1 frustrate you: the engine is calibrated so that even a top-percentile lineup runs the table only 2% to 5% of the time. Winning the tournament makes you CAMPEÓN and that is already a serious run; INVICTO is, by design, a feat on the level of what the Dominican Republic did in 2013. That is why the Daily Challenge exists: identical rolls for everyone, so merit gets measured for real.

8. Badge hunting for collectors

  • Giant Slayer (champion with 5 or more players rated under 60) and No Stars Needed (champion without a single 80+ player) are best hunted in Free Play by accepting humble rosters on purpose.
  • Around the World (12 different countries) demands discipline: refuse to repeat a flag even when the player is tempting.
  • Single Vintage (all 12 from one edition) is easiest in 2023 or 2026, the editions with the most teams in the game.
  • Golden Shutout (win the final without allowing a run) is more luck than pitching: in any game, the losing team scores no runs 1 time in 20, no matter how good either staff is. Your part is to reach the final with your best team and win it; about 1 in 20 of the finals you win ends in a shutout.
